AN650 light controller

Detect a capacitive switch's rising edge and step a lamp through off, 50% and 100%.

On this page
  • The brief
  • Parts allowed
  • Inputs and outputs
  • What a correct circuit must do

The brief#

From AN650: Touch-activated light controller:

When a user touches a capacitive switch, the controller will detect the rising edge and advance the light to the next intensity level - from off, to 50%, to 100%, and back to off.

The load-bearing word is edge. The same page spells out how the first chip finds one:

By storing the previous reading from the capacitive switch in acc, the first microcontroller can detect a transition from low (0) to high (100).

That is a comparison against the previous reading, not a read of the current level. A build that just watches the switch and advances the lamp whenever it reads high will keep climbing for as long as a touch is held, which is not what the brief asks for: one touch, one step, however long it is held.

What a correct circuit must do#

switch is touched three times below, each held for six time units with five units released in between. This is the timeline the workbench's Verify button checks a build against.

A - marks a time unit the brief doesn't pin to an exact instant - it falls inside the first few units of a touch, while the edge is still being detected. Every other cell is exact, including the runs of repeated values while a touch is held: lamp must sit still there, not keep advancing.
